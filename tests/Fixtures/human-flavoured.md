# The Tuesday Repair Log

I keep a text file for the things that broke this week and what I did about
them. It is not a system. It is a list, and I update it on the train.

Tuesday, 14:10. The kettle in the office has stopped boiling properly. I have
logged this four times now and it has been fixed twice, which means the third
report was the one that worked and nobody told the person who fixed it. I have
started copying the number onto the kettle itself.

Tuesday, 16:55. A customer's invoice arrived with our old VAT number on it,
four months after we changed it. Bookkeeper, BR-204, is handling it. The
template still lives in the shared drive and there are apparently four copies.
I have deleted three of them. This is the kind of thing that takes an hour a
month and I have stopped pretending it will fix itself.

Wednesday, 09:30. Rain. The bike light is intermittent. I have a spare in the
drawer under the desk. It was not in the drawer under the desk.

Wednesday, 11:15. Priya asked whether the supplier portal really needs a
password reset every ninety days, and I said I would check, and I did not check,
and now it is on the list. Ninety days is not a security policy I understand. I
would rather it were six months and I would rather it were not at all, but it is
not my call and I am not going to pretend it is.

Wednesday, 13:00. Lunch with Sam about the summer thing. Nothing to report.

Thursday, 08:45. The spreadsheet that tracks the spreadsheet is finally gone. Two
years of quarterly files in a folder called `final_final_v3`, and the person
who made most of them has left. We have kept 2019 because legal asked once and
never again.

## What I actually think

I do not think any of this matters. The kettle and the VAT number are the same
problem and the problem is that nobody owns the small stuff, so I do, badly, on
a train, in a text file that only I can read.

The invoice was the one that stung, because that one costs money and lands on
somebody's desk who did not cause it. I have written the portal password thing
up properly and put it in the drawer with the spare light, which is where
things go in this office.
